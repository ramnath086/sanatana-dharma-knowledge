The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, one of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas in four parts (Brāhma, Madhyama, Pratisarga, Uttara) and ~28,000 verses, narrated by Sūta to the sages at Naimiṣāraṇya. Known for its "future" (bhaviṣya) orientation — prophecies, royal genealogies of future dynasties (Maurya, Gupta, Harsha, medieval Indian rulers, and even British period references), the Sūrya-vaṃśa and Candra-vaṃśa lineages, the Sāyaṇācārya's solar worship, the Kṛṣṇa-līlā, the Tulasī-māhātmya, the Rāma-nāma-māhātmya, the Kāśī-māhātmya, the Gayā-śrāddha, and numerous vrata-kathās. The only Purāṇa with extensive historical/prophetic content on post-Vedic Indian dynasties. Controversial for its late interpolations; the Pratisargaparvan is considered a later addition.
