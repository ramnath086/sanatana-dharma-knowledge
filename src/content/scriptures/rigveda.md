The Ṛg Veda (Ṛgveda Saṃhitā) is the oldest extant Indo-European text (c. 1500–1200 BCE), the first of the four Vedas, containing 1028 hymns (sūktas) in diverse meters. It preserves the oldest attested Indian religious poetry addressed to the Vedic deities (Indra, Agni, Varuṇa, the Ādityas, the Viśvedevas). The Ṛg is cited as the 'earliest testimony' (sākṣat) of the Dharmaśāstras and the source of the Gāyatrī-mantra (3.62.10). Its ten Mandalas (books) are organized around the cosmic order (ṛta); the Ṛg is the foundation of the Vedic ritual (yajña) and of later Indian philosophy (e.g. the puruṣa-sūkta of the tenth Mandala).
