Mandala V of the Ṛg Veda, the 'Aindra-mandala,' preserves hymns to Indra (the Vedic king of the gods and the destroyer of Vṛtra), the Maruts, and the Āditya Varuṇa. The Indra-hymns (especially the 'Hymn to Viśve Devāḥ' and the famous 'Indra-vad') portray Indra as the wielder of thunder and the conqueror of the mountain and the serpent. This Mandala is the locus of the early Vedic 'heroic' theology that makes the sacrifice (yajña) and the soma-drink the condition of victory over chaos.
