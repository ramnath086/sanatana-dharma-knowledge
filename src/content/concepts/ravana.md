Rāvaṇ(a) (रावण, 'the captive') is the ten-headed (daśa-sira) king of Laṅkā, the Rākṣasa who abducts Sītā and is the central antagonist of the Vālmiki Rāmāyaṇa. His ten heads are traditionally the ten organs (indriyas) and the ten directions, the symbol of pride (aṭṭā) and the 'over-reacher' who, despite his learning (śāstra) and boons (vara), is undone by lust (kāma) and the curse (śapatha). The Rāmāyaṇa stages his defeat (vijaya) as the triumph of dharma (dharma) over the demonic (āsura).
