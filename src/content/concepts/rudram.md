The Śrī Rudram (Rudra-praṇālī) is the most celebrated hymn to Rudra-Śiva in the Śukla (white) Yajurveda, occurring as the Taittirīya Saṃhita (VS) 4.1–10 and its Āraṇyaka supplement (the 'Rudram' / 'Ṛgvidhāna'-like 'Rudrāṣṭnama'). It is a long, intricate praise in the form of a dialogue (the 'namo' invocations), expanding from the first aṇu-verse ('namo ... Rudrāya') through progressive rounds (the 11 and 111 repetitions of the namo), culminating in the famed 'Nīlamegha' (dark-cloud) description. It is the chief text recited in the Rudra-abhiseka / Chāturmāsa rites, and the Rudra-adhyāya is counted as one of the three 'mahā-adhyāyas' of the Yajurveda.
