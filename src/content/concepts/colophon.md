The colophon (nāmadheya) is the scribe's termination of a manuscript, typically recording the scribe (likhita), date (saṃvatsara), place (desha), patron (dattarāja), and invocation of merit (puṇya). Colophons reveal transmission history: they name regional scripts (e.g. Grantha, Devanāgarī, Malayalam), date works to specific reigns or festivals, and expose corrections, multiple scribes, and ownership chains (pustakapratāpti). In printed editions colophons record the editor's source and corrections; for critically-edited Sanskrit texts the colophon is a datum for the apparatus and provenance.
