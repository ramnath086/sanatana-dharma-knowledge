Jñāna (ज्ञान) is discriminative knowledge that removes the veil of māyā and reveals the identity of ātman and Brahman, the means of liberation in the jñāna-mārga. Śaṅkara locates jñāna in the teachings (upaniṣad-vākya) transmitted through the paramparā and validated by śravaṇa (listening), manana (reflection), and nididhyāsana (meditative absorption). The Gītā's jñāna-yoga (chapters 2, 13, 15) distinguishes higher (para) knowledge (Brahma-jñāna) from lower (aparā) knowledge (the five elements and Vedas). In the Buddhist tradition jñāna is non-conceptual; in Vedānta it is counted among the three Puruṣārtha sādhāṇas alongside karma and bhakti.
