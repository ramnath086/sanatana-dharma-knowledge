Vairāgya (वैराग्य) is dispassion or detachment from sense-objects and worldly fruits, the preliminary qualification (sādhana) for the knowledge-life of Advaita and the threshold of renunciation. Śaṅkara's Vivekacandrikā distinguishes vairāgya as the wish to abandon sense-enjoyment for the Supreme. The Gītā (2.64–66) pairs vairāgya with ārjava (straightforwardness) as a twin means to mokṣa. Traditional texts distinguish śuddha vairāgya (in monks) from sāmānya (householder detachment in action). It is cultivated through the memory of death (maranasaṁsmṛti), impermanence-reflection (anitya-vihāra), and satsaṅga.
