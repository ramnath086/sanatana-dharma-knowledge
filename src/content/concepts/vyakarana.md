Vyākarakṣa (grammar) is the Vedāṅga of linguistic analysis, systematized by Pāṇini in the Aṣṭādhyāyī (c. 4th c. BCE) in 399 sutras with the A bhikṣuṇī and Śiva sutras. Its economy (samāsa, sandhi) defines the phonological operations (ādeśa, āgama, parasa) and the derivational morphology (kāraka, karma, kriya) that secure correct recitation and interpretation of the Vedas and the literature. The Mahābhāṣya of Patañjali defends the grammar against Mīmāṃsā exegesis and Buddhist critiques; the tradition of commentaries (Kātyāyana, Pūjyayi) remained the locus of Indian linguistic theory until modern times.
