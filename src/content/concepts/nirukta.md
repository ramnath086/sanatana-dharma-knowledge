Nirukta, the Vedāṅga of etymology and interpretation traditionally ascribed to Yāska (6th c. BCE), explains the meaning of archaic and ambiguous words, especially Vedic hymn-names and vocables, by deriving them from roots (dhātu) and analyzing compounds (samāsa). Its method (nirukta) balances the phonetic (śabda) with the semantic (artha) and distinguishes the established usage (kāraṇa) from the speculative (tātparya-vacana). It preserves some of the oldest native linguistic thinking (the 'three' vowels, the derivation of proper names) and anchors the Brāhmana and Prātiśākhya interpretation of the Vedic corpus.
