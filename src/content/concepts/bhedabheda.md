Bhedābheda ('difference and non-difference') holds that the individual self (jīva) and the supreme (Brahman/Īśvara) are simultaneously distinct and non-distinct depending on the epistemic standpoint. Classical sources are the Udgīta of the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad, the Vedānta-siddhānta, the Viśiṣṭādvaita of Rāmānuja and the Dvaita-influenced Vedānta of Nimbārka and Vallabha, and the Gauḍīya school of Jīva Gosvāmī, who grounds bhedābheda in the Rāsa-līlā. It opposes both Advaita's identity and Dvaita's difference, articulating relationality (saṃbandha) as a substantive structure of reality.
