Bhakti (भक्ति) is devotion — love, surrender, and reverence toward a personal deity (īśvara) or guru — and is the central practice of the Vaiṣṇṇava, Śākta, and Śaiva devotional traditions. Originating in the early-tamil Āḻvār/Nayanmar poetry (c. 6th c. BCE–5th c. CE) and the Brahmanical epics, bhakti was systematized by Rāmānuja (śaraṇadhīna) and particularly by Chaitanya Mahāprabhu (mahā-bhāgavatānvīta). The Gītā (12) addresses the bhakta who fixes the mind on the supreme person. Bhakti is cultivated through hearing (śravaṇa), chanting (kīrtana), worship (puja), and surrender (praṇāma/prapa), distinguishing it from jñāna- and karma-mārga.
