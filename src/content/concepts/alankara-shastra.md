Alaṅkāra-śāstra is the theory of poetic ornamentation, systematized in the work of Bhāma Kṣapa (Bhāma) and culminating in the treatises of Uṃāśambhu (Kāvyas, 10th c.) and the later tradition of Jñāna Kṣapa (12th c.). It enumerates the thirty-one figures of speech (alaṅkāras) such as alliteration (anuprāsa), simile (upamā), exclamation (utprekṣā), and the ten poetic defects (doṣas) and thirty-eight merits (guṇas, e.g. śleṣa) that render a composition aesthetically effective (rasa). The śāstra distinguishes primary (bādhāra) and figurative (gīya) ornaments and prescribes their deployment to evoke the eight rasas (ṣāḍhārane) defined in the Nāṭyaśāstra (cf. rasa-theory).
