The Mahāpurāṇa ('Great Purāṇa') refers to the eighteen major Purāṇas (e.g. Vishnu, Śiva, Devi, Bhāgavata) distinguished from the Upapurāṇas by their broader scope, earlier strata, and fuller treatment of the pañcalakṣaṇa (five features). They preserve the theological synthesis of the śruti-smṛti, genealogies (vaṃśa), cosmology (kalpa), and the avatars of Viṣṇu across myth and history. The Bhāgavata is the exemplar. The Mahāpurāṇas are also a textual group: their redactors (the 'five brāhmaṇas') and regional recensions vary; the Padma and Vāyu preserve variant genealogies used by historians of Indian religious art.
