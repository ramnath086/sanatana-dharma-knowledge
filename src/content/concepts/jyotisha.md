Jyotiḥśāstra (astronomy/astrology) is the Vedāṅga of celestial timekeeping (tithi, muhūrta, rāhu, yoginī, nakṣatra) regulating the ritual calendar and election of auspicious moments (muhūrta). The Taittirīya (Āraṣṭabhāṣini) and the Brāhmapurāṇa codify the nakṣatra (27/28) system, the motion of the planets (graha), and the timing of festivals; the Siddhānta tradition (Āryabhaṭa, Brahmagupta) later integrated Greek and Islamic elements. As a Vedāṅga it is a liminal discipline: timekeeping (the 'outer' jyotiṣa) for ritual and the horoscopic/astrological (the 'inner') for divination and life-timing.
