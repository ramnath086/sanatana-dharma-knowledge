Paleography (lekhā-śāstra) is the discipline of dating and localizing scripts from their letter-forms (akṣara) and writing habits (ligatures, rephas, anusvāra). In India the script-family (Brāhmī → Gupta → Devanāgarī, Grantha, Tibetan, Śāradā, etc.) is branched by the 4th c. BCE, with each script regionally diversified (e.g. Nāgārjunakonda, Gilgit). Paleography anchors datation of undated manuscripts and inscriptions; standards (Girard, Salomon) correlate letter-form evolution with political chronology and monastic centres of scribal production.
