Jīvanmukti (जीवन्मुक्ति, 'liberation while living') is the highest attainment in Advaita and the other Vedāntas, in contrast to videhamukti (liberation after death). Śaṅkara defines it as the removal of the false superimposition (adhyāsa) by the means of knowledge (jñāna) — the realized sage (jīvanmukta) who is liberated while still embodied and moves in the world as the 'child of fortune' (saṃsāra). The text (Gītā 2.72, the Kathopananiṣad) describes the jīvanmukta as the 'great-souled' (mahātmā) whose conduct (caraṇa) transcends the guṇas.
