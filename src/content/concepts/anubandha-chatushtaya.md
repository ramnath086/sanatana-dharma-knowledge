The Anubandha-catuṣṭaya ('the four-fold adjunct') is the traditional opening inquiry of a Vedānta work posed by Śaṅkara's bhāṣyas: adhikārin (who is fit to hear), viṣaya (what is taught — Ātman-Brahman), prayojana (why — mokṣa), and sambandha (the relationship of teacher-text-and-student). These four conditions ensure that jñāna-yoga rests on a qualified hearer, a valid scripture (śruti), a competent teacher (guru), and a clear purpose (mocana). The framework recurs in the Brahma-sūtras (the 'prasthāna' of reason) and the Gītā's gradual method.
