Śikṣā is the Vedāṅga of phonetics and phonology, the science of correct pronunciation (uccāraṇa) and the accents (udātta, anudātta, svarita) essential for valid Vedic recitation. Its texts (the Prātiśākha works attached to each Vedic school) enumerate the fourteenfold (or twentyfold) elements of articulation (varga) and the rules of sandhi, as well as the musical modes (svara) into which the recitation is cast. Distinct recitation lineages (jaṭā, ghana, dhaḍhi, etc.) preserve the phonetic structure; the śikṣā corpus grounds the entire hermeneutic tradition's fidelity to the sonic (śabda) dimension of the Veda.
