Māyā-vāda ('the doctrine of illusion') asserts that the phenomenal world is māyā (unreal appearance) in relation to the absolute Brahman. The term is used polemically by the Bauddhas and as a label for Advaita by its critics (Madhva, the Mīmāṃsakas), who object that it trivializes the reality of the world and the agency of the deity. Advaitins prefer the more precise 'vivarta-vāda' (apparent transformation): māyā is anirvacanīya (indescribable), the superimposition (adhyāsa) of the real upon the unreal, removed by jñāna rather than denied as inert.
