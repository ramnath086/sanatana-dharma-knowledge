Emendation is an editorial intervention proposing a reading different from all extant witnesses when the tradition appears corrupt (homoeoteleuton, dittography, scribal errors). Classical criteria (the Latin 'difficilior potior' — prefer the harder reading, transcribe divine author) evaluate plausibility: a lectio difficilior, an internal coherence with context, and agreement with the author's style. In Sanskrit the apparatus also weighs metrical (chandas) and grammatical (vyākaraṇa) constraints. Emendations are siglated (* abbreviatio) and debated in secondary literature rather than silently imposed.
