Strī-dharma (stri-dharma, 'the law/duty of women') is the Dharmaśāstra corpus governing the status and rites of women: the girl-child (kumārī), the maiden (virgin), the wife (patnī), and the widow (vidhava). The Dharma-sūtras and Smṛtis (Manu 5-6, Yājñavalkya 4.95) prescribe the 'three lifetimes' (triguṇa) of a woman's dharma — mītā (moderation), svādhyāya (self-study), and saṃgama (union). Modern scholarship distinguishes these textual prescriptions from the diverse regional and historical actual practice (vyavahāra) of living women.
