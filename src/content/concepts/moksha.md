Mokṣa (मोक्ष) is liberation from the cycle of birth and death (saṃsāra) and the cessation of karmic bondage, the final of the four puruṣārthas. The Darśanas offer divergent formulations: Ādi Śaṅkara defines it as jīvanmukti — the realization that ātman is Brahman, accomplished in life; Rāmānuja locates it in eternal loving service (prema) to Nārāyaṇa; Madhva in eternal distinction between soul and God. The Gītā presents jñāna, karma, and bhakti mārgas as convergences toward mokṣa. Mokṣa is variously temporal (jīvanmukti) and ultimate (paramukti), with Śaiva and Buddhist variants as ātura-jñāna and nirvāṇa respectively.
