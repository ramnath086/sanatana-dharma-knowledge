Ṛṇa (ऋण) is debt or obligation, a fundamental principle in Indian thought whereby the individual owes debts (ṛṇa) to the cosmos, gods, sages, ancestors, and fellow humans. The concept is articulated in the Gītā (17.14) and elaborated in the Ṛg Veda (e.g., the Gāyatrī-ṛṇa) and the Dharmaśāstras. Repayment (ṛṇa-nivṛtti) is through progeny (putrārtham), study (śrāddha), and ritual (yajña), culminating in liberation when the debts are discharged. The Buddhist conception of anattā rejects ṛṇa as a permanent self-debtor, while the Jain aṇuvañcana counts ṛṇa-kṣaya (debt-diminishment) among the attributes of a liberated being.
