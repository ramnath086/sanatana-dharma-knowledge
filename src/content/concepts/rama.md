Rāma (राम) is the hero (puruṣottama) of the Vālmiki Rāmāyaṇa, the ikṣvāku prince exiled to the forest, husband of Sītā and brother of Bharata and Lakṣmāṇa. The epic (Bāla to Yuddha) stages the ideal of dharma through the conflict between duty and family (his abandonment of Sītā). His coronation after the war establishes the ideal of Rāma-rājya — righteous kingship — and he is later worshipped as the mūrti of bhakti and the exemplar of the ideal son, husband, and king. The Rāmcharitamanas and the regional Rāmāyaṇas preserve his cult.
