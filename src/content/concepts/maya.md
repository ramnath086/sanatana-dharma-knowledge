Māyā (माया) is the creative power or projective illusion that conceals Brahman as the phenomenal world, the central problem of Advaita Vedānta. Śaṅkara describes māyā as anirvacanīya ('indescribable') — like rope mistaken for a snake — neither existent nor non-existent. The Gītā (9.38–40) attributes the world's appearance to māyā as the womb of the worlds; the doctrine grounds Advaita's epistemology (only Brahman is real, all else is mithyā dependent on Brahman) and the doctrine of adhyāsa (superimposition).
