Vivarta-vāda — the theory of apparent transformation — is Advaita Vedānta's doctrine that the world is an illusory appearance (vivarta) superimposed on Brahman, not a real change. Śaṅkara argues in the Brahma-sūtra Bhāṣya (1.4.2) that since Brahman is immutable, the manifold cannot be a real transformation; the rope-snake (adhyāsa) illustrates perception imposing form on formless. The doctrine is defended against Buddhist idealism and the Sāṃkhya realism; it grounds Advaita's epistemic hierarchy (śruti over anumāna and pratyakṣa in matters of ultimate reality).
