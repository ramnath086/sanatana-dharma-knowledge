Oṃ (ॐ) is the praṇava, the primordial syllable, the sound-form of the absolute from which the Vedas and all phenomena are said to arise and dissolve. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka and the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣads (oṃ ity etad akṣara) present oṃ as bow, the self as the arrow, Brahman as the target. Its three sounds (a-u-m) correspond to waking, dream, and deep-sleep, and the fourth (parā, turīya) the transcendent state beyond the three. It opens every Vedic and tantric act and is the object of japa and dhyāna.
