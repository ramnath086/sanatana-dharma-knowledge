Codicology is the study of manuscripts as physical artefacts: support (palm leaf, paper, birch bark), layout (margins, pradéli text-blocks), scribal practices (colophon, correction, addition), quire structure (chapters, catchwords), and binding. Descriptive standards (CDLI, MMS, Digital Index of Middle-Eastern Manuscripts) capture material, paleographic, and provenance data. For Sanskrit, the palm-leaf (talapatra/birch) format, the vertical orientation, and the use of yellow or red inks mark regional traditions; the discipline grounds textual criticism by distinguishing scribal from authorial variation.
