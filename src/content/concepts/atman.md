Ātman (आत्मन्) is the innermost self or principle of individuation, the subject-of-experience for whom the Vedic ritual, the Upaniṣadic investigation, and the Yoga path are undertaken. The Upaniṣadic dictum 'tat tvam asi' (Thou art That) sets ātman against the supreme reality, Brahman, and raises the question of identity versus difference answered variously across schools: non-dualistically in Advaita (ātman is Brahman), qualified non-dually in Vaiṣṇṇava and other traditions, and dualistically in Sāṃkhya/Yoga and the Āstika Darśanas. The Gītā describes mokṣa as realization of the imperishable ātman, 'not the destructible body.'
