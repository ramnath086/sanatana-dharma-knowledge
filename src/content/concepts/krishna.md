KṚṣṇa (कृष्ण) is the black (śyāma) avatar of Viṣṇu (Vāsudeva) and the silent author of the Bhagavad Gītā. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa and Harivaṃśa present him as the Vṛndāvana cowherd, the ruler of Dvārakā, and the charioteer of Arjuna. His teaching of līlā (divine play), kāma as divine love, and the synthesis of jñāna, karma, and bhakti in the Gītā make him the pivot of the Vaiṣṇṇava theological tradition. His stories (Govardhana, Kaliya) and his counsel define the bhakti corpus.
