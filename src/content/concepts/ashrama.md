Āśrama (आश्रम) refers to the traditional four-stage scheme of Hindu life: brahmacarya (student), gṛhastha (householder), vānaprastha (retired), and saṃnyāsa (renunciate), the social and spiritual framework of the varṇāśrama-dharma system. The Gītā (6.1–2) treats saṃnyāsa as the crown of the āśrama sequence; the Dharmaśāstras (Manu Smṛti 3–6) elaborate duties (dharmas) of each stage, particularly the householder's support of the sacred (yajña, bhakti, dāna). The āśrama system was historically a means of integrating spiritual aspiration with social duty and is variously reinterpreted in modern reform movements (Rammohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda).
