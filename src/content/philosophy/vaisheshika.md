Vaiśeṣika, systematized by Kaṇāda (c. 6th c. BCE) in the Vaiśeṣika Sūtra, is the school of atomistic pluralism (vaiśeṣika = 'difference'). It classifies reality into seven categories (padārthas): substance, quality, activity, universal, particular, inherence, and (later) non-existence, with the earth-water-fire-air-ether atoms (anu) as the material basis of the cosmos. Liberation (mokṣa) is achieved by right knowledge (pramā) of these categories and the Self's distinctness from the body. The school's later synthesis with Nyāya produced Navya-Nyāya's analytic epistemology; its atomic theory influenced later Jain and Buddhist cosmology.
