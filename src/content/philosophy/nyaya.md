Nyāya, systematized by Gautama (Akṣapāda) in the Nyāya Sūtras (c. 2nd c. BCE), is the school of logical analysis and epistemology. It enumerates four pramāṇas (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) and the four pramāṇa-sādhāraṇas (sadhya, hetu, udāharaṇa, prayogika), the structure of a sound inference (vyāpti), and the sixteen categories (padārthas) culminating in the highest good (paramārtha, mokṣa). The school's logical apparatus (Hemacandra, Vātsyāyana, Ganganāthavādi) became the lingua franca of Indian philosophical debate (śāstra-saṃgrahāṇi) and influenced Vedānta, Buddhism, and Jain logic.
