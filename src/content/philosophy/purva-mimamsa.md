Pūrva Mīmāṃsā (Jaimini), the 'Earlier' exegesis, centres the exegesis (mīmāṃsā) of the Karmasūtras of the Vedas as the fountainhead of dharma. It holds the eternal, authorless (apauruṣeyā) Vedic injunction (śruti) and its ritual prescriptions paramount, defended through a sophisticated theory of language (abhāva-vāda, jāti) and exegesis (six āropas of Vedic interpretation). Whereas the Pūrva stresses ritual efficacy and the eternity of dharma, the Uttara (Vedānta) emphasizes jñāna. Its epistemology, grammar, and theory of scriptural authority deeply shaped Vedānta and the Dharmaśāstra tradition.
