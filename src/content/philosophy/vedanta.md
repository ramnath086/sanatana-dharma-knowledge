Vedānta ('end of the Vedas') denotes the darsana grounded in the Prasthānatrayi — the Upaniṣads (primary), the Bhagavad Gītā (bridge), and the Brahma-sūtras (summary) — systematized from the 8th c. It adjudicates the nature of Brahman and its relation to the world and the self through three great epistemological stances: non-dualism (Advaita, Śaṅkara), qualified non-dualism (Viśiṣṭādvaita, Rāmānuja), and dualism (Dvaita, Madhva), later joined by Śuddhādvaita (Vallabha), Dvaitin (Nimbārka), and the Gauḍīya tradition (Jīva). Its modern history includes the Hindu Renaissance (Rāmana, Vivekananda) and contemporary Advaitic and Neo-Vedānta movements.
