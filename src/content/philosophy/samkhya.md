Sāṃkhya, traditionally ascribed to Kapila, is the dualistic enumeration (siddhānta) of twenty-five tattvas from Puruṣa (consciousness) and Prakṛti (matter). Its emanation schema (Sāṃkhyakārikā of Īśvarakṛṣṇa) unfolds via the three Guṇas from the subtle avyakta through ahaṃkāra to the gross elements and the twenty-four tattvas that constitute experience. It enumerates three kinds of suffering (ādhyātmika, ādhibhautika, ādhidaivika) whose cessation (kaivalya) comes by discriminative knowledge (viveka) of Puruṣa's aloofness from Prakṛti. Yoga (Patañjali) adopts the same metaphysics and adds the practical discipline of abhyāsa-vairāgya.
