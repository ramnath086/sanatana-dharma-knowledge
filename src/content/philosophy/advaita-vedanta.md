Advaita Vedānta, systematized by Ādi Śaṅkara (c. 8th c.), teaches non-duality: ātman is Brahman and all multiplicity is māyā (adhyāsa, apparent super-imposition). Its epistemology (pramāṇa) admits pratyakṣa, anumāna, and śabda (the Upaniṣads), validated by śravaṇa-manana-nididhyāsana. Śaṅkara's Brahmaphilosophy distinguishes the absolute (paramārtha) from the empirical (vyavahārika) and the apparent (prātibhāsika) levels. The tradition's commentarial lineage (bhāṣyas on the Prasthānatrayi) and its polemics with Mīmāṃsā and Buddhism shaped medieval Hindu thought; its modern exponents include the Dashāṅga and Neo-Advaita figures.
