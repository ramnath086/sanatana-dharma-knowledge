Saṃskāra (संस्कार) are the subliminal mental impressions formed by action, experience, and conditioned repetition, the psychophysical substrate of saṃsāric continuity in Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain thought. Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (2.11–18) describe saṃskāras as latent impressions (āśaya) that condition present and future experience; they are the fuel (śakti) of the three kleśas (afflictions) and are exhausted by sustained practice (abhyāsa) and non-attachment (vairāgya). Karmakāṇḍa rites produce saṃskāras of fitness for higher knowledge, while Buddhist Vipassanā counts 84,000 defilements (kammaṭṭhāna) to be dissolved.
