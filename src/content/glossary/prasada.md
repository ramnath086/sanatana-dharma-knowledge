Prasāda (प्रसाद) is the sanctified remnant of a offering (naivedya) distributed to devotees after worship (puja), the 'grace that falls' (prasāda) from the divine. The term also denotes the divine grace (śakti) that descends as food and the 'sweetness' (madhurā) of spiritual experience. The distribution of prasāda (pradakṣina and prasāda) is central to the temple and the household ritual; it is shared as an equalizing social element (no caste) and is held to carry the blessing of the deity (āśirvāda). Its preparation (e.g. coconuts, sweets) is codified.
