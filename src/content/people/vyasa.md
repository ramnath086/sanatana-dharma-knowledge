Vyāsa (or Vyāsa-kṝṣṇa / Vedavyāsa) is the legendary ṛṣi traditionally credited with compiling the countless Vedas into the four Vedas (Samhitās, Brāhmaṇas, Aranyakas, Upaniṣads) and with the massive composition of the Mahābhārata (100,000 ślokas), which contains the Bhagavad Gītā and the Bhāgavata's principal frame. He is also the traditional author-commentator of the Brahma-sūtras (Vedānta-sūtras) and of the Harivaṃśa. As the father (pitṛ) of the kuru lineage and the pupil of the sage Dvaipāyana, he is the archetypal compiler (vyāsa) of the Hindu canon.
