Vallabha (1478–1532), the Ādi of the Vallabha (Kāshapa) Sampradāya, taught Śuddhādvaita ('the sole existent is Viṣṇu, all else mithyā) and the path of kāma (divine love) through the grace of Nārāyaṇa. Regarded as a child-avatāra (bāla-avatāra), he composed the Anuvyākhyāna on the Bhāgavatam and the stutis, and established the sacred geography of Vṛndāvana. His descendants (the Puṣṭi-mārga, the 'path of nourishment' by divine grace alone) and the Vallabha-sampradāya continue in the Braj region, worshipping Nārāyaṇa with the family of divine parents (kalyāṇa-gopāla).
