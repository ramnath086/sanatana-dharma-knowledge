Caitanya Mahāprabhu (1486–1534) was the 16th-century Gaudiya founder who proclaimed the supremacy of krīḍā (congregational chanting) of the names of Kṛṣṇa (nāma-saṅkīrtana) as the yuga-dharma of the Kali age. Born Viṭṭa Haricāṇḍa in Nadia (Bengal), he journeyed across the Ganga basin teaching bhakti as the path of love, and established the rukmiṇā-siddha-saṅgha (devotee community). His theology (jīva-svabhāvika and ādi-svarūpa) is recorded in the Śikṣāṣṭaka, the Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Kṛṣṇa-das Kavirāja) and the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (Rūpa Gosvāmī). The Gaudiya tradition (sampradāya) traces its ādi-guru lineage through him to Nityananda and Advaita Ācārya.
