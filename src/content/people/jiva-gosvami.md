Jīva Gosvāmī (1616–1706) was the pre-eminent ācārya of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava school, the principal architect of its theology, and a scholar-poet of the Vṛndāvan tradition. A disciple of the Six Gosvāmīs' followers, he composed the Sat Sandarbhas (Ṣaṭ Sandarbha) systematizing Gauḍīya doctrine — rasa theory, līlā, the six ālaṅkāras — and commentaries on the Gītā and the Bhāgavata. His kavya and the Jīva-bodhanī constitute a classical synthesis of bhakti and Sanskrit poetics; he is honoured as the bhāṣyācārya (great commentator) of the Gauḍīya line.
