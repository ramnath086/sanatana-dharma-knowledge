Nimbārka (c. 13th–14th c.; 'Keśava Kācyānugraha') is the 13th-century Ādi of the Nimbārka (Kumāra) sampradāya, which teaches the simultaneous oneness and difference of the soul and God (bhedābheda) — Viṣṇu and his energies (śakti) Nārāyaṇa and Lakṣmī — in a dual-aspect monism. The Nimbārka Siddha and the Nimbārka-śataka (attributed) set forth his doctrine of 'the two truths' (sat and asat) and the efficacy of the names (nāma) and surrender (prapatti). The tradition preserves the Mūlamadhyamakāla (the original work on Viśiṣṭādvaita) and a distinctive iconographic tradition of Nārāyaṇa with four arms.
