The Odissi is a classical dance of India's eastern coast, a ritual and narrative dance of the body (kāya-nṛtya) distinguished by the tribhanga (three-bend) posture, the torso-arch (ḍolā) and the side-arm (paṭāka). It is associated with the Jagannātha and the Sūrya traditions — the temple-dance (maṇḍapa) of Puri and the dance-sculptures of the Sun Temple at Konark. Revived in the 20th century by Kelucharan Mohapatra, it is one of the eight classical dance forms, noted for its lyrical (lāsyātana) abhinaya.
