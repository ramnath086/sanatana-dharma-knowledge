Chitrakalā (चित्रकला, 'the art of painting') is the Indian pictorial tradition, from fresco murals (the Buddhist caves of Ajanta and Bagh, the temples of Orissa and Tamil Nadu) and court miniatures (Mughal, Rajput, Pahari, Deccani) to folk and tribal styles (Madhubani, Warli, Gond, Patachitra). Painting is counted among the sixty-four kalās and is grounded in the rasa (aesthetic sentiment) and bhāva (emotion) theory of the Nāṭya-śāstra, linking it to the poetics (kāvya) and the iconography (mūrti) of Hindu and Buddhist art.
