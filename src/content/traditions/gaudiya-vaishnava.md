Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition is the Kr̥ṣṇa-bhakti sampradāya initiated by Caitanya Mahāprabhu (1486–1533) in 16th-century Bengal, who taught the 'yuga-dharma' of congregational chanting of the holy names (kṛṣṇa-nāma-saṅkīrtana) as the prime practice of the age. Its theology centers the dovetailing of the individual soul (jīva) with Kr̥ṣṇa's eternal rasa-līlā (the 'rasa of the gopīs'), expressed through the progressive stages of devotion (sādhana-bhakti → prema, divine love), and is codified in the writings of the Six Gosvāmīs (Rūpa, Sanātana, Jīva, Gopala Bhaṭṭa, Dhāmodara, and Raghunātha dasa). Its centers include the holy dham (sacred geography) of Nabadwip (the birthplace of Caitanya) and Vṛndāvana (the childhood place of Kr̥ṣṇa), and it should not be conflated with the other Vaiṣṇava traditions of India (e.g., the Vallabha school of Rajasthan and the Vaiṣṇava schools of South India).
