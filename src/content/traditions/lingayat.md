Lingayata is the medieval Karnataka tradition of the Liṅga-śiva, reformed and systematized in the 12th century by Basavānī (Basavānī, 1106–1167), Allama Prabhu, and the Veeragāṇa saints. Its hallmarks are the daily worship of the liṅga (the 'universal symbol of Śiva' as the 'body of Śiva') worn always, the egalitarian rejection of caste, the Veera-saṅgīṇa (military-saint) poetry composed in the language of the saints, and the ethics of 'work as worship' (kāyaka). Its distinct institutions include the Piḷḷaṇa mathas and the Śākti-pīṭha (the temple of the Liṅga) of Karnataka, and it should not be conflated with the Śaiva Siddhānta or the other Śaiva traditions of South India.
